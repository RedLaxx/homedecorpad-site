Entryway Bench Ideas: 15 Ways to Add Seating and Storage

A bench is the most underrated piece of entryway furniture. It gives you a place to sit while you put on shoes, a spot to set down a bag and, if you choose well, storage for everything else. In a home with children, a bench is often the difference between shoes in a cubby and shoes in a heap by the door.

This guide has 15 entryway bench ideas, from shoe-shelf and lift-top storage benches to woven benches, mudroom built-ins and a thrifted bench with a new coat of paint. It also covers how to choose the right size and how to style the wall and floor around it. Many ideas work in small and narrow entries, and most cost little. This post is part of our entryway series, so see the complete guide to decorating an entryway for the full plan.

How to choose an entryway bench

Start with the size. Most benches have a seat height of 17 to 19 inches, which is comfortable for putting on shoes, and a depth of 12 to 16 inches. Most are 36 to 48 inches wide, which seats two people. In a narrow entry, look for a depth of 12 to 14 inches, and leave about 36 inches (91 cm) of clear floor in front so people can pass.

Then think about storage. An open shelf below the seat keeps shoes visible and lets air move through, which suits everyday pairs. A lift-top or drawer hides things completely, which is better for gloves, hats and off-season items. Baskets and cubbies sit in between.

Check the build. A bench has to take real weight every day, so look for a solid frame and a stated weight rating, and test it by sitting on it if you can. If you choose an upholstered seat, pick a performance fabric or a cover you can wash, since entries take mud, rain and pets.

Finally, match the style. A bench in the same wood tone as your floor or door looks built in, and a contrasting piece reads as a feature.

1. Choose a bench with a shoe shelf

A bench with an open lower shelf is the simplest way to add shoe storage under a seat. The shelf holds three to six pairs, keeps them off the floor and lets wet shoes dry. Choose one with a slatted or wire shelf so water can drain, and put a small tray under the dirtiest pairs. If the open shelf looks messy, add two baskets and let them hold the extras. Keep the pairs you use every day on the shelf and move the rest to a closet. A light wood bench keeps the look warm, and a painted one blends into the wall.

2. Add a storage bench with a lift-up lid

A lift-top bench hides everything inside the seat, which makes the entry look clean. Use it for gloves, hats, dog gear, folded blankets and off-season shoes. Look for a safety hinge that holds the lid open so it can't drop on small fingers, and check that the cushion on top can be lifted or slid aside easily. Keep the contents sorted with a couple of fabric bins, so you don't have to dig. A lift-top works well in a family entry where hidden storage matters more than showing shoes.

3. Pick an upholstered bench for comfort

An upholstered bench is more comfortable than bare wood and adds softness to a hard-surfaced entry. Linen, bouclé and velvet all look good, but choose a performance fabric if you have children or pets, because it resists stains and wipes clean. Darker colors and subtle patterns hide dirt better than cream. Check the seat for firm foam, since a thin cushion flattens quickly, and look for slim legs, which keep the floor visible and make the entry feel larger.

4. Try a woven or rattan bench

A woven bench adds texture and a relaxed, natural look, and the open weave keeps it visually light. It suits boho, cottage and coastal entries, and it pairs well with baskets and a jute runner. Add a linen cushion for comfort, since bare rattan can be hard and can snag clothing. Check the frame under the weave, because a solid wood or metal frame lasts longer than a thin one. Woven benches are best in a dry entry, so keep them out of direct rain and wipe up wet shoes quickly.

5. Use a slim bench in a narrow entry

In a narrow hallway, a deep bench blocks the way. Choose a slim bench about 12 to 14 inches deep, and push it against one wall so people can pass. Open legs and a light color keep it from looking heavy. If the walkway is still too tight, a wall-mounted fold-down seat or a narrow ledge bench gives you somewhere to sit without taking floor space. Keep the area above clear of anything that sticks out, such as large frames or deep shelves, so the hallway stays easy to use. See our guide to narrow entryway ideas for more.

6. Hang hooks or a peg rail above the bench

Hooks above a bench turn a seat into a complete drop zone. Mount a row of three to five hooks about 60 inches (150 cm) from the floor for adults, and add a lower row at 40 to 48 inches for children. Use a wooden rail for a cleaner look, and fasten it into studs or with proper anchors, since coats and bags are heavy. Choose hooks in brass, black or wood to match the bench. Keep the display to a few items, so the wall doesn't turn into a coat pile.

7. Hang a mirror above the bench

A mirror above a bench makes a small entry feel larger and gives you somewhere to check how you look before you leave. Choose a large, tall mirror, such as an arch or a full-length rectangle, and hang it high enough that you don't bump it when you sit. Center it over the bench, or lean it on the floor beside it. Pick a thin frame in black, brass or wood to match the bench. See our guide to entryway mirror ideas for more shapes and sizes.

8. Layer cushions and a throw

A couple of soft layers make a bench feel like part of the room. Use one or two cushions in solid colors that match the palette, and add a folded wool or cotton throw over one end. A mix of textures, such as linen, wool and a little bouclé, looks collected without being busy. Choose washable covers, since an entry cushion collects dirt faster than a sofa cushion, and use a non-slip pad under any seat cushion so it doesn't slide. Keep it to two cushions and one throw, or the bench stops working as a place to sit.

9. Tuck baskets underneath

Baskets under a bench give you flexible storage that can change as your needs do. Choose two or three that slide in with a few inches to spare, with handles so you can pull them out easily. Use one for everyday shoes, one for scarves, hats and gloves, and one for dog gear or umbrellas. Woven seagrass, rattan and felt all look good. Line the inside with fabric if you store anything that might snag, and keep a small tray under the basket for wet shoes. Matching baskets look calm, while a mix looks more collected.

10. Build a mudroom-style bench with cubbies

A built-in bench with cubbies gives each person in the household a spot for shoes and bags. Use a seat height of about 18 inches, a depth of 14 to 16 inches and a row of hooks above. Open cubbies below hold baskets or bins, and a cushion on top makes the seat comfortable. You can fake the built-in look with an off-the-shelf shelf unit and a custom seat top, then add trim and paint it all one color. It costs far less than a carpenter, and the pieces can come with you if you move.

11. Choose a solid wood bench in a natural finish

A solid wood bench is durable, easy to refinish and good-looking for years. Oak, ash and walnut all work well, and a simple design with tapered legs suits almost any style. A natural or lightly oiled finish shows the grain and keeps the entry warm, and small scratches fade into the look. Keep it simple on top with a tray or a small stack of books. If the bench is for heavy daily use, check the joinery, since screws and dowels hold better than staples. A wood bench also pairs with nearly any rug and mirror.

12. Fit a bench into a nook

A nook is the best place for a bench, since it adds seating without taking walkway space. Use the space beside the front door, an alcove or the area under a window. A built-in window seat with drawers below gives you storage and a cozy spot to sit. Measure the nook carefully, and order a custom cushion if the size is unusual. In a rental, a freestanding bench cut to the nook's width gives a similar look. Keep the surface clear of clutter, so the seat is easy to use and the nook stays calm.

13. Style the bench with a plant and art

A plant and a piece of art make a bench look like part of a designed room. Set a floor plant, such as a snake plant or a ZZ plant, beside the bench, since both tolerate low light. Hang a large framed print above, about two-thirds the width of the bench, or lean one against the wall. Add a small basket under the seat and a tray on top. Keep the colors tied to the rest of the entry, and leave most of the seat open, since the bench still has to hold a person.

14. Rescue a vintage or thrifted bench

A secondhand bench costs less than a new one and often has better construction. Check the frame for wobble, rot and woodworm, then sand it and repaint it in a soft color such as sage, cream or navy. Replace the seat cover with a durable fabric, using a staple gun and new foam if the old foam is flat. A coat of clear sealer helps the finish stand up to shoes. If the bench has a character mark or a worn corner, leave it, since a bit of wear suits a lived-in entry.

15. Define the zone with a rug and sconces

A rug and a pair of sconces turn a bench into a defined entry area, which matters most when the entry opens straight into a living space. Lay a runner or small rug in front of the bench, wide enough that you can put your feet on it while you sit, with a non-slip pad underneath. Hang plug-in sconces on either side, with a warm 2700K bulb, and hide the cords in cord covers painted the wall color. See our guides to entryway rug ideas and entryway lighting ideas for more.

How to style an entryway bench

When you're not sure where to start, work through these five steps.

  1. Add a soft layer. One or two cushions and a folded throw make the seat inviting.
  1. Add hooks above. A short row of hooks gives coats and bags a home.
  1. Add a mirror or art. Hang it above the bench, centered, and high enough that you won't bump it.
  1. Use the space below. Baskets, a shoe tray or a shelf keep the floor clear.
  1. Add a rug. A runner or small rug under the bench defines the zone and catches dirt.

Keep the seat mostly clear. A bench that holds a person is more useful than one buried in decor.

Entryway bench mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a bench that's too deep. In a narrow entry it blocks the walkway, so check the depth first.
  • Wrong seat height. A seat that's too low or too high is awkward for putting on shoes, so aim for 17 to 19 inches.
  • Skipping the weight rating. A bench has to take real weight every day, so check the rating before you buy.
  • Using delicate fabric. Entries take rain, mud and pets, so choose a performance fabric or a washable cover.
  • Weak hooks. Coats and bags are heavy, so fasten hooks into studs or use proper anchors.
  • Piling everything on the seat. Keep it clear so people can sit.
What size should an entryway bench be?

Most entryway benches are 36 to 48 inches wide, 12 to 16 inches deep and 17 to 19 inches tall. In a narrow entry, choose a depth of 12 to 14 inches, and leave about 36 inches (91 cm) of clear floor in front.

How do I add storage to an entryway bench?

Choose a bench with an open shelf, a lift-up lid or drawers, or tuck baskets under a plain bench. Use bins inside to keep small items sorted.

What goes above an entryway bench?

Hooks or a peg rail for coats and bags, a mirror or a large piece of art, and a pair of sconces for light. Keep the wall simple so the hooks stay usable.

Can I use a bench in a small entry?

Yes. Choose a slim bench with open legs, a wall-mounted seat or a bench in a nook, and use wall hooks instead of floor storage. See our guide to small entryway ideas for more.
