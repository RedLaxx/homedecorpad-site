This guide has 15 entryway mirror ideas covering shapes, sizes and placements, from round and arched mirrors to full-length leaners, matching pairs, clusters and mirrors with sconces. It also explains how to choose the right size, hang it at the right height and keep a heavy mirror safe. Most ideas work in small entries too. ## How to choose the right entryway mirror

Start with the size. If the mirror goes above a console, a bench or a cabinet, choose one about two-thirds the width of the furniture below it. A mirror much narrower than that looks lost, and one wider than the furniture looks top-heavy.

Then check the height. Center the mirror near 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor, which is close to average eye level. When it hangs above a table, leave about 6 to 8 inches (15 to 20 cm) between the bottom edge of the frame and the tabletop, so the two read as a group.

Think about shape. Curves, such as circles, arches and organic shapes, soften a space full of straight lines, while rectangles and squares suit a modern, tailored look. Match the frame finish to your hardware, lamp or hooks, so the metals repeat.

Finally, look at what the mirror will reflect. A window, a plant or a lamp doubles the good things in the room, and a pile of coats doubles the clutter. Stand where the mirror will hang and look at what it will show before you drill.

## 1. Hang a round mirror to soften a boxy entry

Entries are full of straight lines: doors, frames, shelves and furniture. A round mirror breaks them up and makes the space feel softer. Hang one above a console, a bench or a row of hooks, and choose a diameter about two-thirds the width of the furniture below it. A thin black or brass frame looks modern, and a wood or rattan frame looks warmer. Center it near 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor. A round mirror also suits a narrow wall, since it doesn't need much width.

## 2. Choose an arched mirror for a modern-classic look

An arched mirror adds height and a gentle curve, and it suits almost any style. Lean a tall one against the wall beside a bench or a console, or hang a smaller one above a table. Thin brass, black and wood frames all work. The arch draws the eye up, which helps a low ceiling feel taller. A full-length arch also works as a dressing mirror, so it earns its space. Choose a size in proportion to the furniture beside it, and secure a leaning mirror to the wall for safety.

![Tall arched brass mirror leaning against the wall beside an oak bench](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-02.jpg)

## 3. Use a tall rectangular mirror for height

A tall, vertical rectangle emphasizes the height of a narrow wall. It works well in a hallway or a small entry where there isn't room for a wide mirror. Hang one centered above a slim console, or on the wall beside the front door at eye level. A thin black metal frame looks sharp and modern, and a wood frame looks warmer. Make sure the mirror is tall enough to show you from the chest up at least, and hang it so the center sits near 57 inches (145 cm).

![Tall vertical rectangular mirror with a thin black frame above a slim console](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-03.jpg)

## 4. Try an organic-shaped mirror

A mirror with a soft, irregular outline, like a pebble or a blob, works as a sculpture as well as a mirror. It breaks up the straight lines in a modern entry and suits minimal and Scandinavian styles. Choose a thin or frameless edge so the shape does the talking, and hang it above a console or a bench. Because the shape is unusual, keep the surrounding decor simple, with one lamp and one object. The mirror becomes the art, which means you can skip a separate piece above the furniture.

![Large frameless pebble-shaped mirror above an oak console in an entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-04.jpg)

## 5. Make a statement with a sunburst mirror

A sunburst mirror, with rays radiating around a round center, is decorative enough to act as the focal point of the whole entry. Brass and gold versions suit traditional and glam styles, and wood or black versions suit boho and modern ones. Hang it above a console with simple styling, since the mirror is doing a lot already. Keep the center of the mirror near 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor, and check the weight, because many decorative mirrors are heavier than they look.

![Large gold sunburst mirror above a dark wood console with brass candlesticks](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-05.jpg)

## 6. Hang a vintage or antique mirror

An old mirror adds history and character that new ones can't. Look at antique shops, thrift stores and online marketplaces for gilt, carved wood or painted frames. Slight spots and foxing in the glass are part of the charm, and they give a soft, aged look. Check that the frame is sturdy and that the hanging hardware is secure, and replace the wire or hooks if they look weak. Pair a vintage mirror with simple, modern furniture, so the mix looks deliberate.

![Antique gilt-framed mirror above a sage console with dried flowers in an entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-06.jpg)

## 7. Lean a full-length mirror against the wall

A full-length mirror makes a small entry feel much bigger and gives you a proper outfit check on the way out. Lean one against the wall, with the bottom resting on the floor or a low shelf, and choose one at least 60 inches tall. It needs no hanging hardware, which suits renters. Secure the top to the wall with a discreet strap or bracket, since a tall mirror can tip, especially in homes with children or pets. Place it where it reflects a window or a plant, not the front door.

![Full-length mirror leaning against the wall of a small entryway beside a stool](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-07.jpg)

## 8. Hang a matching pair for symmetry

Two identical mirrors side by side make a long wall look balanced and intentional. Hang them above a long console or a bench, with a gap of about 2 to 4 inches between them, and center the pair on the furniture. Round or arched mirrors in a matching finish look especially good. The pair doesn't have to be expensive, but it should be identical, since near matches look like mistakes. Use a level and paper templates, because uneven spacing is easy to see when there are only two pieces.

![Matching pair of round brass mirrors above a long white console with two lamps](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-08.jpg)

## 9. Group small mirrors into a cluster

A cluster of small mirrors works like a gallery wall that also bounces light. Mix round, oval and arched mirrors in a consistent finish, such as brass or black, and hang them in a loose group above a bench or a console. Vary the sizes and keep the gaps even, about 2 to 3 inches. Lay the arrangement on the floor first and trace each mirror on paper to test the layout. A cluster is a good way to fill an awkward wall, and it's forgiving, since one mirror can be moved without spoiling the whole group.

![Cluster of small round brass mirrors on the wall above an entryway bench](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-09.jpg)

## 10. Hang a mirror above a console table

The classic placement is still the best one. A mirror above a console gives the table a backdrop, doubles the light from a lamp and makes the whole vignette feel finished. Choose a mirror about two-thirds the width of the table, center it, and leave 6 to 8 inches between the bottom of the frame and the tabletop. Keep the styling simple, with a lamp, a tray and one object. Our guide to [hanging a mirror above a console table](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-mirror-above-console-table) goes into sizing and spacing in more detail.

![Wood-framed mirror centered above a white console with a lamp and a tray](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-10.jpg)

## 11. Place a mirror opposite a window

A mirror that faces a window carries daylight deep into the entry, which helps a dark or windowless space feel brighter. Hang it on the wall opposite the window, or on an adjacent wall at an angle where it catches the light. The bigger the mirror, the more light it bounces. Check the reflection at different times of day, since glare from direct sun can be harsh, and move the mirror slightly if it dazzles you. A plant or a view of the sky in the reflection adds a nice effect.

![Large mirror on the wall opposite a window bouncing daylight into an entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-11.jpg)

## 12. Hang a mirror above a bench with hooks

A mirror above a bench turns a seat into a complete drop zone. Choose a tall one, such as an arch or a rectangle, and hang it high enough that you don't bump your head when you stand up from the bench. Put hooks beside it, not directly below it, so coats and bags don't cover the glass. A frame in black, brass or wood that matches the hooks ties the wall together. Center the mirror over the bench or just off center if the hooks take up one side.

![Tall arched mirror above an entryway bench with a hook rail holding a coat beside it](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-12.jpg)

## 13. Hang a mirror beside the front door

A mirror on the side wall, right by the door, is where you'll actually use it. It lets you check your hair and your outfit as you step out, without crowding the main wall. Hang it at eye level, with the center near 57 inches (145 cm), and put a small shelf or a hook below for keys. A narrow mirror works well in a tight spot. Keep it clear of the door swing, and check that you can see yourself without stepping into the walkway.

![Mirror on the side wall beside a front door with a small shelf and key hook below](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-13.jpg)

## 14. Mount a mirror on the back of the door

The back of the front door is unused space, and a mirror there needs no wall at all. A full-length mirror mounted on the door gives a small entry light and a way to check your outfit, and it frees the walls for hooks and shelves. Choose a lightweight mirror with shatter-resistant backing, and use hardware rated for the door and the weight, since a heavy mirror can strain the hinges. Check with your landlord first if you rent, or use an over-the-door mirror that hooks on without drilling.

![Full-length mirror mounted on the back of a front door in a small entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-mirror-ideas/entryway-mirror-ideas-14.jpg)

## 15. Flank a mirror with sconces

A mirror between two sconces looks polished and puts warm light right where you need it. Hang a round or arched mirror above a console, with a plug-in sconce on each side, centered at about 60 to 66 inches (152 to 168 cm) from the floor. Use a warm 2700K bulb, and hide the cords in cord covers painted the wall color. The mirror doubles the glow, so even small bulbs make the entry feel bright. Put the sconces on a dimmer or a smart plug, so the light can drop low in the evening. ## How to hang a heavy mirror safely

Mirrors are heavier than they look, and a falling one is dangerous, so take a few minutes to hang it properly.

Weigh it first. Check the weight on the label or with a bathroom scale, and choose hardware rated for at least that weight, with some margin. A heavy mirror needs two hooks, a French cleat or a rated wall anchor in a stud, and a light one can use a picture hook or an adhesive strip made for the weight.

Use two people. One holds the mirror while the other checks the level and the height.

Mark before you drill. Use painter's tape and a pencil to mark the center and the hanging points, and use a level.

Secure leaners. A tall mirror that leans on the floor should be fixed to the wall at the top with a strap or bracket.

Check now and then. Hardware can loosen over time, especially near a door that slams.

## Entryway mirror mistakes to avoid

- Choosing a mirror that's too small. A tiny mirror above a long console looks lost, so aim for two-thirds the width.
- Hanging it too high. Center it near 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor so people can actually see themselves.
- Reflecting clutter. Check what the mirror shows before you hang it, and move the pile.
- Ignoring the weight. A heavy mirror needs proper hardware and a stud or a rated anchor.
- Not securing a leaner. A tall mirror can tip, so fix it to the wall with a strap.
- Skipping the light. A mirror in a dark corner reflects the dark, so put it opposite a window or a lamp.
- Mixing too many frames. Choose one or two finishes and repeat them across the entry.

## Frequently asked questions

### How high should I hang an entryway mirror?

Center it near 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor. If it hangs above furniture, leave about 6 to 8 inches (15 to 20 cm) between the bottom of the frame and the surface.

### What size mirror should go above an entryway console?

Choose a mirror about two-thirds the width of the console, centered above it. A taller mirror suits a narrow wall, and a wider one suits a long table.

### What shape of mirror is best for a small entry?

A large round or arched mirror softens the space and reflects light. A tall full-length mirror also makes a small entry feel bigger. Choose a thin frame so it doesn't look heavy.

### Where should a mirror go in the entryway?

Above a console or a bench, opposite a window, or on the side wall beside the door. Choose a spot where it reflects light or a plant and not clutter.
