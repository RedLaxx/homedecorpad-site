Good entryway table decor solves both problems. It gives everyday items a home and makes the surface look good. This guide has 15 ideas for styling a console table, from lamps and trays to plants, baskets and lidded boxes, plus a simple formula you can use on any table. Most of the ideas cost very little, and you can mix a few of them. This post is part of our entryway series, so see the [complete guide to decorating an entryway](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/how-to-decorate-an-entryway) for the full plan.

## What makes a good entryway table setup

An entryway table has two jobs. It has to work, with a clear spot for keys, mail and bags, and it has to look good, since it's the first thing most people see. The best setups do both without a lot of objects.

Start with scale. Slim console tables for tight entries are often 10 to 14 inches deep, so leave walking room and choose objects that don't hang over the edge. A lamp, a tray and one or two objects are enough on a narrow table, and a wider table can take a few more.

Think in layers. Put something tall at the back, such as a lamp or a mirror, something medium in the middle, such as a vase or a stack of books, and something low at the front, such as a tray. Varying the heights is what makes a table look styled instead of cluttered.

Finally, leave space. About a third of the surface should stay bare, so you have somewhere to set a bag or a package.

## 1. Start with a table lamp

A lamp is the most useful object on an entryway table. It gives the space a warm glow in the evening, adds height and makes the table feel like part of a room instead of a shelf by the door. Choose one that's roughly 24 to 30 inches tall, so it's in proportion to a standard console table, and pick a slim base if the table is narrow. A linen or cotton shade in cream or white throws soft light, and a 2700K bulb keeps the color warm. Run the cord behind a table leg or along the wall so it doesn't dangle in view.

## 2. Add a catch-all tray

Keys, wallets, sunglasses and loose change need a place to land, or they end up on every flat surface in the house. A shallow tray gives them one. Choose a material that suits the entry: wood or leather for a warm look, marble or metal for a cleaner one, a woven tray for texture. Pick one with a low rim so items are easy to grab, and keep it small enough to leave room for decor around it. Empty it once a week, since a tray that overflows looks like clutter.

![Leather catch-all tray with keys and sunglasses on a walnut console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-02.jpg)

## 3. Hang a mirror above the table

A mirror above the table is the classic entryway pairing. It bounces light around, helps a small entry feel larger and gives you a place to check how you look on the way out. Choose one about two-thirds the width of the table, and center it so the middle sits near eye level, around 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor. A round mirror softens a boxy room, and an arched or rectangular one suits a modern look. Our guide to [entryway mirror ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-mirror-ideas) covers shapes and sizes in more detail.

## 4. Style a vase of flowers or dried stems

Fresh flowers make an entry feel welcoming the moment someone walks in. A small bunch is enough: three to five stems in a glass or ceramic vase, with the leaves stripped below the waterline so the water stays clean. If you don't want to replace them every week, dried flowers, pampas grass or branches last for months. Keep the arrangement narrower than the table is deep, so it doesn't spill onto the surface, and set it to one side so you still have a clear patch for keys and mail.

![Vase of white tulips and eucalyptus on an entryway console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-04.jpg)

## 5. Stack a few books

A short stack of two or three books adds height and a bit of personality. Choose hardcovers in colors that suit the palette, such as cream, tan, black or muted green, and set them flat with the largest on the bottom. Put a small object on top, like a candle, a small bowl or a brass dish, to turn the stack into a base. Books also lift a lamp or a plant a couple of inches, which helps if a piece looks too short for the table. Stick to books you like looking at, since you'll see them every day.

![Stack of cream hardcover books with a brass dish and candle on an oak console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-05.jpg)

## 6. Tuck baskets under the table

The space below the table is usually wasted. A pair of baskets under it gives you hidden storage for shoes, scarves, dog leashes and bags, and it adds texture. Choose baskets that slide in with a few inches to spare, and pick ones with handles so they're easy to pull out. Woven seagrass, rattan or felt all work. Line the inside with fabric if you plan to store anything that might snag. In a narrow entry, a low, flat basket tucks under more easily than a deep one.

![Two woven seagrass baskets stored under a white entryway console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-06.jpg)

## 7. Add a plant

A plant adds life and a soft shape to a hard surface. In a dim entry, choose a plant that tolerates low light, such as a snake plant, a ZZ plant or a pothos. A trailing plant spilling over the edge of the table softens the straight line, and a small upright plant fits when space is tight. Use a cover pot in ceramic, terracotta or woven fiber to match the palette, and set the plant on a saucer so water doesn't mark the table. If the entry gets no light at all, a good faux plant is a fair choice.

![Trailing pothos in a terracotta pot on a narrow oak entryway table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-07.jpg)

## 8. Hang or lean art above the table

Art above the table finishes the arrangement. If you don't want a mirror, hang a large framed print, or a small group of frames about two-thirds the width of the table. Or skip the hanging and lean a large frame against the wall, which looks relaxed and lets you change it whenever you like. Choose a piece that suits your palette, and hang it so the bottom edge sits about six to eight inches above the table. ## 9. Style a matching pair

Symmetry makes a table look calm and finished. Use a pair of matching lamps, candlesticks or vases, one at each end, with a tray or bowl between them. It suits a long table against a wall with a mirror centered above it. The pair doesn't have to be expensive, but it should be identical, since near matches look like mistakes. Keep the center clear for a tray, and don't fill the whole surface, or the symmetry makes the table look formal and cramped.

![Matching brass candlesticks at each end of a white entryway console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-09.jpg)

## 10. Add candles and holders

Candles add warmth and give a table a finished look, even when they aren't lit. Use two or three in different heights, such as a tall taper in a brass holder, a pillar on a small dish and a short votive. Choose beeswax or unscented ones near the front door, since a strong scent can clash with whatever is cooking. Never leave a lit candle unattended, and keep it away from curtains and mail. For a worry-free option, flameless LED candles flicker convincingly and are safe near drafts.

![Three candles in brass and ceramic holders on a dark wood entryway console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-10.jpg)

## 11. Place one sculptural object

One interesting object can anchor a whole table. Choose a ceramic vase with an unusual shape, a carved wood bowl, a stone sphere or a small sculpture, and give it room to stand alone. It works especially well in a minimal entry, where a lamp and one object are all you need. Pick something with a texture or color that picks up another element in the room, such as the mirror frame or the rug, so the table feels connected to the space around it.

![Sculptural cream ceramic vase on a minimal oak entryway console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-11.jpg)

## 12. Swap in seasonal decor

Changing one or two items through the year keeps the entry fresh without a rebuild. Keep the lamp, tray and mirror in place, and change the small things: branches and pumpkins in fall, evergreen and pine cones in winter, tulips in spring, shells or greenery in summer. Store the seasonal items in one box so the swap takes ten minutes. ## 13. Mount a key and mail rack above the table

A key and mail rack on the wall keeps the table surface clear. Choose a wooden board with a row of hooks and a small shelf, or a wall-mounted letter holder with a slot for mail. It gives keys and envelopes a home and stops them from covering the table. Mount it beside the mirror where it's easy to reach, and keep it to a few hooks. Pair it with a tray on the table for the things that don't hang.

![Wooden key and mail rack above a white entryway console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-13.jpg)

## 14. Tuck a stool underneath

A small stool or ottoman tucked under the table adds seating without taking floor space. Pull it out to sit while you put on shoes, and push it back when you're done. Choose one that fits fully beneath the table, with its top lower than the tabletop. A woven, upholstered or wooden stool all work. It also gives you a place to set a bag while you find your keys. In a very narrow entry, a bench may suit better, so see our [entryway bench ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-bench-ideas).

![Round cream stool tucked under a slim oak entryway console table](/assets/uploads/entryway-table-decor-ideas/entryway-table-decor-ideas-14.jpg)

## 15. Hide clutter in lidded boxes

Lidded boxes hide the things nobody wants to see: chargers, dog treats, spare keys, gloves and loose receipts. Choose two or three in wood, woven fiber or lacquer, and stack or line them on the table. They look like decor, so the surface stays tidy even when the contents change. Label the inside if several people share the entry. Keep the boxes small enough that you don't pile things on top, and empty them every few weeks.

## A simple formula for styling any entryway table

When you're not sure where to start, work through these five steps.

1. Add height. A lamp, a mirror or a tall vase gives the table a high point.

2. Add something useful. A tray or a lidded box gives everyday items a home.

3. Add something living. Flowers, branches or a plant soften the surface.

4. Add one personal object. A framed photo, a small sculpture or a stack of books keeps the table from looking like a showroom.

5. Leave space. Keep about a third of the table clear for bags, packages and the things you drop in a hurry.

Use odd numbers of objects, vary the heights, and repeat one or two colors from the room. You can build most good tables from just these five pieces.

## Entryway table decor mistakes to avoid

- Too many small objects. A table covered in tiny items looks cluttered, so use fewer, slightly larger pieces.

- No clear space. If you can't set a bag or a package down, the table stops being useful.

- Wrong scale. A small lamp or mirror on a long table looks lost, and a deep table in a narrow entry blocks the walkway. Our guide to [narrow entryway table ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/narrow-entryway-table-ideas) covers slim options.

- Forgetting light. A dim entry feels unwelcoming, so add a lamp or a sconce.

- Ignoring the space underneath. Baskets or a stool turn it into storage.

- Matching everything. A mix of materials, such as wood, metal and ceramic, looks more collected than a set.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do you put on an entryway table?

Start with a lamp, a tray for keys and a vase or plant. Add a stack of books or a sculptural object, and hang a mirror or art above the table. Keep about a third of the surface clear.

### How do you style a narrow entryway table?

Keep to three items: a slim lamp, a small tray and one object. Choose objects that don't hang over the edge, and use the wall above the table for a mirror or a key rack so the surface stays clear.

### How high should a mirror hang above an entryway table?

Center the mirror near eye level, around 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor, and leave about six to eight inches between the bottom of the frame and the table. A mirror about two-thirds the width of the table looks balanced.

### How do I keep an entryway table from looking cluttered?

Limit the table to three to five objects, use a tray and lidded boxes to contain small items, and empty them regularly. Leave about a third of the surface bare.
