A welcoming entry doesn't need a lot of space or money. It needs a few things done well: warm light, somewhere to put your things, a piece or two you love and a bit of life. This guide has 15 entryway decor ideas, from paint and mirrors to lighting, rugs, plants, baskets, wallpaper and personal touches. Each idea says what to do and what to watch for, and many cost very little. This post is part of our entryway series, so see the [complete guide to decorating an entryway](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/how-to-decorate-an-entryway) for the full plan.

## What makes an entryway feel welcoming

Most welcoming entries share four things.

Warmth comes first. It's the color on the walls and the light in the room. Soft, warm tones and a warm bulb make a space feel kind before anyone notices a single object.

Order comes next. If shoes, bags and mail have nowhere to go, no amount of decor will help. Fix the function first, with hooks, a seat and a tray, and then decorate around it.

Then character. One or two pieces you actually like, such as a vintage mirror, a favorite print or a bench you found secondhand, stop the entry from looking like a showroom.

Finally, life. A plant, fresh flowers, a woven basket or a subtle scent gives the space texture and a sense that someone lives there.

You don't need all 15 ideas below. Pick the ones that match what your entry is missing.

## 1. Choose a welcoming color palette

Color sets the mood before a single object does. In a small entry, soft, warm colors like warm white, cream, pale greige or sage keep the space open, while a deeper shade such as navy or forest green makes it feel intentional and cozy. Pick the wall color first, then choose two accent colors for the rug, art and accessories. Test a large sample in morning and evening light, since entries often get little daylight and colors shift. A satin or eggshell finish wipes clean, which matters in a space where coats, bags and dirty shoes brush the walls every day.

## 2. Paint the inside of the front door

The inside of the front door is a wall you see every day, and it's often left builder white. Paint it a color that contrasts with the walls, such as deep green, charcoal, soft blue or warm terracotta, and it becomes a focal point for almost no cost. Use a satin or semi-gloss paint made for doors, since it takes a lot of handling, and swap the hardware for brass or black if it looks tired. In a rental, check your lease first. If you can't paint, hang a large mirror or a piece of art on the door instead, if the door and hinges can carry the weight.

![Front door painted deep forest green inside a warm white entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-02.jpg)

## 3. Hang a mirror that does double duty

A mirror is the fastest way to make an entry feel bigger and brighter. Choose one about two-thirds the width of the furniture below it, and center it near 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor. Hang it opposite a window or a light source so it bounces daylight around. A round mirror softens a boxy space, and a large arched or full-length mirror leaning against the wall adds drama. Check what it reflects before you hang it, because a mirror that shows a pile of shoes makes things worse. Our guide to [entryway mirror ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-mirror-ideas) covers shapes and sizes.

![Large round brass mirror above a white console beside a window in an entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-03.jpg)

## 4. Add warm, layered lighting

Many entries are lit by one harsh overhead bulb. Add a table lamp or a pair of plug-in sconces, and swap the overhead bulb for a warm 2700K one, and the space feels welcoming at once. Layer the light: one source at head height or higher, and one lower, like a lamp on a console. Put at least one source on a dimmer or a smart plug, so it can glow low in the evening. Hide cords in cord covers painted the wall color, or run them behind furniture. See our guide to [entryway lighting ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-lighting-ideas) for more.

![Warm sconces and a lit table lamp above a console in an entryway at dusk](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-04.jpg)

## 5. Lay a runner or rug to anchor the space

A rug defines the entry, catches dirt and adds color and texture underfoot. Choose a low-pile rug, about 1/4 inch or less, so the front door clears it, and put a non-slip pad underneath. In a narrow entry, a runner that leaves a border of bare floor looks tidy, and in a wider one, a rug big enough to stand on works better. Washable and indoor-outdoor rugs handle wet shoes, and a pattern hides dirt. A rug can also set your palette: pull one or two of its colors into your art and accessories. See our [entryway rug ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-rug-ideas) for sizes and materials.

## 6. Style a console table vignette

A console table gives you a ready-made stage. Build a small arrangement with a lamp for height, a tray for keys, a vase of flowers or dried stems, and a stack of books or one sculptural object. Keep about a third of the surface clear so you can set down a bag or a package. Odd numbers of objects and a mix of heights look natural. If the table is narrow, stick to three items. Our guide to [entryway table decor](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-table-decor-ideas) has 15 ways to style one.

## 7. Add a bench or stool

A place to sit makes an entry feel useful and welcoming, and it gives you a spot to put on shoes. Choose a bench with a seat height of 17 to 19 inches, and look for one with a shelf or baskets underneath for shoes. In a narrow entry, a slim bench about 12 to 14 inches deep works best, and a small stool that tucks under a console is a good option for very tight spaces. Add a cushion or a folded throw for comfort, and keep the seat mostly clear. See our [entryway bench ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-bench-ideas) for 15 options.

## 8. Create a gallery wall or hang one large piece

The wall above your console or bench is prime space for art. A gallery wall of mixed frames adds personality, and one large piece makes a calmer statement. Keep the group about two-thirds the width of the furniture below, with about two inches between frames, and center it near eye level. Use a consistent color or frame finish to hold a mixed group together. Plan the layout with paper templates before you hammer anything. Our guide to [entryway wall decor](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-wall-decor) has more ideas for the walls around your entry.

![Gallery wall of black and oak frames above a slim entryway console](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-08.jpg)

## 9. Bring in greenery

A plant adds life and softens the hard lines of furniture and doors. In a dim entry, choose a plant that tolerates low light, such as a snake plant, a ZZ plant or a pothos. A tall plant in a corner by the door adds height, and a trailing plant on a console softens the edge. Use cover pots in ceramic, terracotta or woven fiber to match your palette, and put a saucer under each one so water doesn't mark the floor. If the entry gets no light at all, a good faux plant is a fair choice, and fresh or dried stems in a vase work well too.

![Tall snake plant in a basket and a trailing pothos in a bright entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-09.jpg)

## 10. Add woven baskets for texture and storage

Woven baskets bring warmth and hide small clutter at the same time. Put a pair under a console or bench for shoes and scarves, use a larger one by the door for umbrellas or a throw, and hang a few flat ones on the wall as art. Seagrass, rattan and felt all look good, and baskets with handles are easier to use every day. Mix sizes and keep the colors natural, so they add texture without competing with your palette. Line the inside with fabric if the contents might snag.

![Woven baskets under an entryway bench and flat woven baskets on the wall above](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-10.jpg)

## 11. Choose hooks that look like decor

Hooks are the most practical part of an entry, so make them look good. Choose a wooden peg rail or a row of brass or black hooks that suits your style, and keep the display to five or six items so it doesn't turn into a coat pile. Mount adult hooks about 60 inches (152 cm) from the floor, and add a lower row at 40 to 48 inches for children. Fasten the rail into studs or use proper wall anchors, since coats and bags are heavy. A rail painted the same color as the wall almost disappears, and a wood one adds warmth.

![Wooden peg rail with black hooks holding a coat, tote bag and straw hat](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-11.jpg)

## 12. Use wallpaper or paneling for character

A single treated wall can give a plain entry real character. Peel-and-stick wallpaper in a small floral, a soft stripe or a tonal geometric suits renters, and painted beadboard or board-and-batten paneling adds depth in a house you own. Use it on one wall, or on the lower half of the wall up to a chair rail, and keep the rest of the walls plain. Measure carefully and order extra wallpaper for pattern matching. Choose a washable finish, since entry walls take scuffs from bags and coats.

![Sage board-and-batten paneling behind a console and mirror in an entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-12.jpg)

## 13. Mix in a vintage or thrifted piece

One secondhand piece adds history and keeps an entry from looking like a catalog. Look for an antique mirror, a wooden bench, a brass tray, a small cabinet or a framed painting at thrift stores, estate sales and online marketplaces. Check the frame for wobble and damage, then clean, sand or repaint if you need to. A scuffed finish is fine, since a bit of wear suits a space that gets daily use. Pair the piece with something new and simple, so the mix looks deliberate.

![Vintage wooden bench and antique gilt mirror in a modern entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-13.jpg)

## 14. Add a signature scent

Scent is the part of a welcome people notice without seeing it. A reed diffuser, a small candle or a bowl of dried lavender on the console gives the entry a gentle, consistent smell. Choose something light, such as linen, eucalyptus, cedar or citrus, since a strong scent can clash with whatever you're cooking. Never leave a lit candle unattended, and keep it away from curtains and mail. If anyone in the household is sensitive to fragrance, use flameless options or fresh flowers instead.

![Reed diffuser and candle on a tray on an entryway console beside eucalyptus](/assets/uploads/entryway-decor/entryway-decor-14.jpg)

## 15. Add something personal

The pieces that tell people who lives here are what make an entry feel like a home. Display a framed family photo, a child's drawing, a small collection of travel souvenirs or a piece of art you love. Group them on the console or wall, and rotate them now and then so the display stays fresh. Keep the frames to one or two finishes, so the mix looks collected and not random. One or two personal objects are enough, since the rest of the entry still has to work as a place to drop your keys.

## A simple checklist for a welcoming entry

If you only have an afternoon, work through these six steps.

1. Clear what doesn't belong. Move anything that doesn't live in the entry somewhere else.

2. Add light. A lamp or sconces with a warm bulb change the whole feel.

3. Hang a mirror. It brightens the space and gives you a place to check yourself.

4. Lay a rug. It anchors the space and catches dirt.

5. Add one living thing. A plant or a vase of stems is enough.

6. Add one personal object. A photo, a print or a souvenir makes it yours.

## Entryway decor mistakes to avoid

- Decorating before fixing function. If shoes and bags have no home, add hooks, a seat and storage first.

- Using harsh light. A single bright overhead bulb makes any entry feel cold, so add a warm lamp.

- Choosing furniture that's too big. A bulky piece blocks the walkway, so leave about 36 inches (91 cm) clear.

- Covering every surface. Leave some empty space so the pieces you chose can be seen.

- Matching everything. A mix of wood, metal, ceramic and fabric looks more collected than a set.

- Hanging art too high. Center pieces near eye level, around 57 inches from the floor.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I put in my entryway?

At minimum, a place to hang coats, a place for shoes, a surface for keys and mail, a mirror and good light. Add a rug, a plant and one or two decorative pieces to finish it.

### How do I decorate a small entryway?

Use wall space first, with hooks, a large mirror and art, and choose slim furniture such as a narrow console or a stool that tucks under. Keep the colors light and the objects few. See our guide to [small entryway ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/small-entryway-ideas) for more.

### How do I make my entryway look welcoming?

Start with warm light and a warm color palette, add a mirror and a rug, then add something living and something personal. A clear spot for keys and shoes helps as much as any decor.

### What colors work best in an entryway?

Warm white, cream, greige and sage keep a small entry open, while deep green, navy or charcoal add drama in a larger one. Choose a washable satin or eggshell finish, and test samples in your own light.
