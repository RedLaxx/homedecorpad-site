Kids jungle wall decor works because the theme scales so easily. You can go all in with a painted mural, or keep it quiet with two framed leaf prints and a woven basket. Either way, the walls carry the idea, so the furniture can stay plain and the room still reads as a jungle or safari nursery.

This guide has 15 jungle nursery and playroom wall ideas, from renter-friendly decals to a hand-painted accent wall. Each one comes with notes on where it works best and what to watch for. Most of them also suit a toddler's bedroom, since a jungle theme tends to age more gracefully than a character theme. Pick one idea and build around it, or combine two or three that share a color palette.

## Before you pick a jungle theme

Start with the palette. Jungle rooms look best when the greens vary: sage, olive, deep forest and a lighter leaf green, with a warm accent like terracotta, mustard or sand. Choose one main wall color and two accents, then repeat them across your decor. Bright primary colors can work in a playroom, but a calmer palette is easier to live with in a nursery where a baby needs to sleep.

Think about scale next. Large, simple shapes read well from across a room, while small prints look best when grouped. One oversized leaf usually has more impact than ten tiny ones.

Safety comes before style. Anchor shelves and heavy frames to the wall, keep cords, strings and long hanging pieces out of reach of a crib or a climbing child, and avoid decor with small detachable parts at toddler height. If a product comes with placement guidance from the manufacturer, follow it. For paint or wallpaper in a nursery, look for low-VOC or water-based products and let the room air out before the baby moves in.

## 1. Paint a jungle mural on one accent wall

A hand-painted mural is the strongest version of jungle nursery wall decor, and it doesn't need to be complicated. Paint a base in soft sage, then layer large leaves in two or three greens, with a few simple animals peeking out. If you can't paint freehand, project a design onto the wall and trace it. Choose the wall behind the changing table or the play area, so the busiest design sits where you'll use it instead of directly over the crib.

## 2. Use peel-and-stick jungle wallpaper

Removable wallpaper gives you the mural look without the paint, which makes it a good fit for rentals. Tropical leaf patterns and tonal animal prints both work. Measure the wall carefully, order a little extra for pattern matching, and test a small piece first, since adhesives behave differently on textured or freshly painted walls. Use it on one wall only. A fully papered room can feel heavy, especially in a small nursery.

![Child's bedroom with tropical leaf peel-and-stick wallpaper on one wall](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-02.jpg)

## 3. Add removable jungle wall decals

Decals are the cheapest way to start, and they're easy to change as your child's tastes shift. Look for large leaf sets and animal groups instead of tiny scattered stickers, which tend to look messy. Apply them in clusters: a tree trunk with branches, a vine climbing a corner, a row of animals along the baseboard. Matte finishes look more like paint and less like stickers. Smooth the wall first and press from the center outward so the edges stay flat.

![Playroom wall with large removable jungle decals of a tree, vines and animals](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-03.jpg)

## 4. Hang a gallery wall of framed jungle animal prints

A small gallery wall of framed animal wall art suits a nursery where you want the theme without covering the whole wall. Use three to five prints in matching frames and hang them close together with even gaps. Mix animal portraits with a leaf print so the group doesn't feel like a zoo. Above a dresser, keep the lowest frames a few inches off the surface. In a child's room, choose acrylic instead of glass.

![Gallery wall of framed jungle animal and leaf prints above a nursery dresser](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-04.jpg)

## 5. Choose large botanical leaf prints

If you want a jungle room that still feels calm, use oversized leaf art. Monstera, banana leaf and fern prints look more like decor and less like a theme, so they stay relevant as the room moves from nursery to big-kid bedroom. Hang two matching prints side by side, or one large print above a rocking chair. Natural wood frames and a warm white wall keep the look light.

![Two large monstera leaf prints above a rocking chair in a nursery](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-05.jpg)

## 6. Paint leaf shapes and an arch

You can suggest a jungle without painting a full scene. Paint a large arch in sage behind the crib, then add leaf shapes in darker greens along one edge. Tape the outlines with painter's tape, test the colors with a sample pot, and finish in an afternoon. The result looks modern, works well in a neutral room, and is easy to paint over later.

![Painted sage arch with dark green leaf shapes on a nursery wall behind a wooden crib](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-06.jpg)

## 7. Hang a macramé or woven wall piece

Texture keeps a jungle room from looking flat. A macramé hanging in cream or natural fiber adds softness above a bed or reading chair. Choose one with short fringe and a solid dowel, and hang it well out of reach of a crib, since long strands can be a hazard for young children. A leaf-shaped or feather-shaped piece fits the theme without adding more color.

![Cream macramé leaf wall hanging above a child's reading chair](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-07.jpg)

## 8. Turn woven baskets into wall art

Flat woven baskets and rattan trays make inexpensive jungle wall decor. Group three to five in different sizes, mix light and dark fibers, and hang them in a loose cluster above a bookshelf or play table. They add texture, they're lightweight, and they pair well with greenery. Use removable hooks rated for the weight, and check that nothing hangs low enough for a toddler to pull down.

![Cluster of woven rattan baskets on a playroom wall above a bookshelf](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-08.jpg)

## 9. Mount felt or wooden animal heads

Wall-mounted animal heads made from felt, wood or paper are a common pick for jungle nurseries because they add dimension without the weight of real mounts. A giraffe, elephant or zebra in muted colors works well. Hang one as a focal point, or hang three at slightly different heights. Choose lightweight materials, secure them with proper hooks, and keep them above reach.

![Felt giraffe, elephant and zebra wall heads on a sage nursery wall](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-09.jpg)

## 10. Use a fabric tapestry or banner

A soft fabric tapestry with a leaf or animal pattern covers a lot of wall quickly and can soften the sound in a hard-surfaced room. Linen or cotton in earthy tones looks relaxed. Hang it from a wooden rod with two removable hooks. A pennant-style banner with leaf shapes is a smaller option for a reading corner.

![Linen leaf-pattern tapestry hung from a wooden rod in a child's bedroom](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-10.jpg)

## 11. Install animal peg hooks

In a playroom, wall decor can also do a job. Wooden animal-head hooks hold backpacks, hats and dress-up clothes at a height a child can reach. Mount them low, and fix them to the wall with proper anchors, since kids will hang their full weight on a hook. They also teach tidying habits better than a pile on the floor.

![Wooden animal wall hooks holding a backpack and hat at child height](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-11.jpg)

## 12. Add a jungle growth chart

A growth chart turns a wall into something your family uses. Choose a tall wooden ruler or canvas chart with a jungle design, such as a giraffe stretching up one side, and mark your child's height on each birthday. It works as decor now and a keepsake later. Mount it with the base at floor level so the measurements are accurate, and write the date next to each mark.

![Wooden giraffe growth chart on a nursery wall with height marks](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-12.jpg)

## 13. Hang a safari animal map

A world map with animals placed by continent suits a playroom or a toddler's room, and it gives kids something to point at and talk about. Pick a map in soft greens and tans so it matches the rest of the theme. Frame it, or use a canvas version, and hang it at a child's eye level instead of an adult's.

![Framed animal world map above a playroom reading nook](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-13.jpg)

## 14. Style wall shelves with animal figures

Floating shelves let you display a few wooden animals, a plush toy and picture books with the covers facing out. They put the theme at child level and double as storage. Use sturdy shelves, anchor them into studs or with rated wall anchors, and keep heavier items on the lowest shelf. A deeper green wall behind the shelves makes light-colored pieces stand out.

![Floating shelves on a green wall with wooden animals and picture books](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-14.jpg)

## 15. Finish with a jungle night light

Soft lighting completes a jungle room. A plug-in wall sconce shaped like a leaf, or a small projector that casts leaf shadows on the wall, gives a nighttime glow without a harsh overhead light. Choose a low-heat LED bulb in a warm color, and run cords along the wall in cord covers so nothing dangles near a crib. Test the brightness at bedtime and dim it if the room feels too stimulating.

![Leaf-shaped wall light casting warm leaf shadows in a nursery at dusk](/assets/uploads/kids-jungle-wall-decor/kids-jungle-wall-decor-15.jpg)

## Jungle wall decor mistakes to avoid

- Too many colors. Stick to two or three greens and one warm accent, and repeat them.

- Decorating every wall. One feature wall with quieter walls around it looks calmer and costs less.

- Hanging everything at adult height. In a playroom, put at least some pieces where a child can see and touch them.

- Leaving hazards near the crib. Keep cords, long fringe and heavy frames out of reach.

- Going too babyish. Leaf prints and natural textures last longer than cartoon characters, so add the animals through smaller pieces.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best jungle wall decor for a nursery?

For most nurseries, an accent wall with a mural or removable wallpaper, plus a small gallery of framed animal prints, gives the strongest look with the least clutter. Keep anything with cords or small parts out of reach of the crib.

### How do I decorate a jungle nursery on a budget?

Start with peel-and-stick decals, a few printed leaf prints in thrifted frames and woven baskets. Paint is also cheap, so a painted arch or a few leaf shapes costs very little.

### Does a jungle theme work for both boys and girls?

Yes. Jungle is a neutral theme. Stick to greens, creams and warm earth tones, and the room suits any child. Add stronger color through small accents later.
