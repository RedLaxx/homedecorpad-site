This guide has 13 entryway cabinet ideas, from slim shoe cabinets and cane-door cabinets to wall-mounted units and built-in looks, plus tips on choosing the right size and organizing the inside. Many work in small entries, and most can be styled to look like furniture instead of storage. ## How to choose an entryway cabinet

Start with the space. Measure the width and the depth you have, and leave about 36 inches (91 cm) of clear floor in front so you can walk past and open the doors. If the entry is narrow, look for units about 7 to 12 inches deep, and consider sliding doors or tilt-out drawers that don't swing into the path.

Then think about what you're hiding. Shoes need ventilation and a flat or tilted rack. Bags and coats need height and hooks. Small items like gloves and chargers need drawers or bins. Count what you store now and add a little extra room, since storage fills up faster than you expect.

Look at the build. Solid wood and good plywood usually stand up to wet boots and daily use better than thin particleboard, and a wipeable finish helps in a messy entry. Check the weight rating on shelves and drawers, and look for adjustable shelves so you can change the layout later.

Finally, match the style. A cabinet in the same wood tone as your floor or door looks built in, while a contrasting color makes it a feature.

## 1. Choose a slim shoe cabinet with tilt-out drawers

A slim cabinet with flip-down drawers is the most efficient way to hide shoes in a tight entry. Each drawer tilts open to show a row of pairs, then closes flat, so the front looks like a plain set of doors. Many are only about 7 to 12 inches deep, which makes them workable in a narrow hallway. Check the number of pairs it holds before you buy, since a tall unit holds more than it looks. Choose a color that matches the wall so the cabinet blends in, or a warm wood finish so it looks like furniture. Anchor tall units to the wall with an anti-tip strap.

## 2. Use a console-style cabinet with doors

A cabinet that looks like a console table gives you storage and a surface in one piece. Choose one about 30 to 34 inches tall with doors in front, so you can set a lamp, a tray and a mirror above it as you would on a normal console. The closed doors hide shoes, bags and baskets, and the top stays free for decor. It suits a mid-size entry where you want a good-looking piece but can't give up storage. Pick doors with soft-close hinges, since they're quieter and won't slam when you're rushing out. ## 3. Pair a low cabinet with a cushion for seating

A low cabinet, about 18 inches tall, can double as a bench. Add a fitted cushion or a folded throw on top, and you have a seat for putting on shoes with hidden storage underneath. Choose a cabinet with a flat, sturdy top, and a cushion with a non-slip backing so it doesn't slide. It works best where a full bench would take too much room, since a cabinet is often shallower. Check the weight rating first, and don't use a lightweight unit for seating. For more seating ideas, see our [entryway bench ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-bench-ideas).

## 4. Use a sideboard in a larger foyer

If you have a wide foyer, a sideboard gives you generous hidden storage and a long surface for decor. Look for two or four doors with an adjustable shelf inside, so you can fit boots, bags and baskets. A wooden sideboard in oak, walnut or a painted finish suits a traditional or a modern home. Hang a large mirror or art above it, and put a lamp at each end. Leave about 36 inches (91 cm) of clear floor in front, so the doors can open and people can pass.

![Wide walnut sideboard with a large round mirror in a spacious foyer](/assets/uploads/entryway-cabinet-ideas/entryway-cabinet-ideas-04.jpg)

## 5. Choose cane or rattan doors

Cane and rattan doors keep clutter out of sight while letting air move through, which helps if the cabinet holds shoes. The woven texture adds a natural, relaxed look that suits boho, cottage and modern styles. You can see a little of what's inside, so keep the contents tidy, or add a fabric lining behind the cane to block the view. Look for solid wood frames and tight weaves, since cheap cane can sag. Pair the cabinet with a jute runner and a ceramic lamp to finish the look.

![Natural oak entryway cabinet with woven cane doors and a lamp on top](/assets/uploads/entryway-cabinet-ideas/entryway-cabinet-ideas-05.jpg)

## 6. Mount a floating cabinet on the wall

A wall-mounted cabinet keeps the floor clear, which makes a small entry feel larger and easier to clean. Hang one above a bench, or at a comfortable height with open space below for a basket or a pair of boots. Use anchors that suit your wall type, and check the weight rating, since shoes and bags are heavier than they look. A slim floating cabinet in white, or in a color that matches the wall, almost disappears. Keep the bottom edge high enough to clear a tall bench or the top of a basket.

![Slim white wall-mounted cabinet above a small wooden bench in an entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-cabinet-ideas/entryway-cabinet-ideas-06.jpg)

## 7. Add a tall cabinet for coats and bags

If you have wall space but no closet, a tall cabinet can act as one. Choose a unit with hooks inside the doors for coats and shelves for bags and hats, with doors that close the whole thing off. A hanging rod needs about 20 inches of depth for standard hangers, while hooks inside the doors work in a shallower unit. It's a good answer for apartments and older homes without a coat closet. Anchor it to the wall for safety, and keep the top mostly empty, or add one basket, so it doesn't look crowded.

![Tall white entryway cabinet with one door open showing coats and baskets](/assets/uploads/entryway-cabinet-ideas/entryway-cabinet-ideas-07.jpg)

## 8. Create a built-in look

Built-in cabinets look custom and use the full height of the wall. You can fake the look by lining up two or three off-the-shelf cabinets, adding a shared top surface and trim, and painting the whole run in one color. Leave a gap in the middle for a mirror or a row of hooks. It costs much less than hiring a carpenter, and you can take the pieces with you if you move. Check that the cabinets are level and tight to the wall before you fasten them together, since small gaps show once the paint is on.

![Three white cabinets with a shared wood top and trim for a built-in look in an entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-cabinet-ideas/entryway-cabinet-ideas-08.jpg)

## 9. Choose a cabinet with a mirrored door

A full-length mirror on the cabinet door gives you storage, a place to check your outfit and extra light in one piece. It also makes a small entry feel bigger by reflecting the space. A tall, slim cabinet with a mirrored front fits nearly any narrow entry. Place it where it reflects something worth seeing, such as a window or a plant, and not a pile of coats. Wipe fingerprints off every few days, since a mirror shows everything, and check the mounting, because glass doors are heavy. ## 10. Paint the cabinet a bold color

A cabinet is a small block of color you can change easily, which makes it a good place to try one. Sage green, deep navy, charcoal or warm terracotta all look good against white walls. Sand and prime the surface first, use a cabinet-grade paint or enamel, and let it cure fully before you load it with shoes and bags. Keep the hardware simple in brass or black so the color stays the focus. If you rent, paint a freestanding cabinet instead of the wall, and you can take it with you when you go.

![Sage green entryway cabinet with brass handles, a lamp and white flowers](/assets/uploads/entryway-cabinet-ideas/entryway-cabinet-ideas-10.jpg)

## 11. Mix closed doors with open shelves

A cabinet with doors below and open shelves or cubbies above gives you the best of both. Keep shoes and bags behind the doors, and use the open section for a few baskets, a plant and the things you want to reach quickly, like gloves or an umbrella. The open section also keeps a tall cabinet from looking heavy. Style it with only a few items, so it looks planned and not like a pile of things that didn't fit. Baskets with lids or fabric liners hide the contents while keeping the look soft.

![Oak entryway cabinet with closed doors below and open cubbies with baskets above](/assets/uploads/entryway-cabinet-ideas/entryway-cabinet-ideas-11.jpg)

## 12. Use the space under the stairs

If your entry sits near a staircase, the space underneath is usually wasted. A custom or modular cabinet fitted under the stairs can hold shoes, coats, a vacuum and seasonal items, all behind doors. Pull-out drawers work well in the lower, deeper part, and a hinged panel can cover the shallow end where the stairs come down. Measure the angle carefully, and hire a carpenter if you need a custom fit. It's one of the biggest storage gains you can get without adding any floor area.

## 13. Style the top so the cabinet looks like furniture

A cabinet looks like furniture when the top is styled. Add a lamp, a tray for keys, a small plant, and a mirror or art on the wall above, using the same layering you'd use on a console table. Keep the objects fewer than you think you need, so the cabinet still reads as clean, and leave a clear area for a bag or a package. A mirror above the cabinet also makes the whole unit feel lighter. ## How to organize the inside of an entryway cabinet

A cabinet only hides clutter if the inside stays organized. A few habits keep it that way.

Group by use. Put the things the household uses every day, such as everyday shoes and a main bag, at the front or at hand height, and move seasonal items, like boots and rain gear, to the back or the top.

Use containers. A shallow bin for gloves and hats, a tray for wet shoes and a small basket for dog leashes and umbrellas stop items from spreading across the shelf.

Use the doors. Small hooks or an over-the-door rack inside the cabinet hold keys, dog leashes and tote bags without using shelf space.

Reset weekly. Take two minutes at the end of the week to put shoes back, empty the trays and remove anything that doesn't belong. See our guide to [entryway organization ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-organization-ideas) for more systems.

## Entryway cabinet mistakes to avoid

- Choosing a cabinet that's too deep. In a narrow entry, a bulky unit blocks the walkway, so check the depth against the space before you buy.

- Ignoring door swing. Doors that hit a wall, a bench or each other are annoying every day, so use sliding doors or tilt-out drawers in tight spaces.

- Skipping ventilation. Closed shoe storage can get musty, so choose cane or slatted doors, or air the cabinet out regularly.

- Overfilling. A cabinet crammed to the top is hard to use, so leave some space on every shelf.

- Forgetting to anchor. Tall cabinets can tip, especially in homes with children, so fasten them to the wall.

- Leaving the top bare or crowded. A lamp, a tray and one or two objects make a cabinet look finished without adding clutter.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an entryway cabinet?

An entryway cabinet is a closed storage piece for the entry, such as a shoe cabinet, a console cabinet or a sideboard. It hides shoes, bags and small items behind doors and often has a flat top for decor.

### How deep should an entryway cabinet be?

Slim shoe cabinets with tilt-out drawers are often 7 to 12 inches deep, and general storage cabinets are commonly 12 to 16 inches deep. In any entry, leave about 36 inches (91 cm) of clear floor in front.

### What can I store in an entryway cabinet?

Shoes, bags, scarves, gloves, umbrellas, dog leashes, keys, mail and cleaning supplies for the entry all fit well. Keep everyday items easy to reach, and store seasonal ones at the back or the top.

### How do I make an entryway cabinet look less bulky?

Choose a color that matches the wall, hang a mirror above it, keep the top simple, or switch to a wall-mounted unit that leaves the floor visible.
