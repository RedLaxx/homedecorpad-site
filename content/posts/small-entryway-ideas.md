The trick is to work with light, walls and restraint. This guide has 15 small entryway ideas that make a tiny entry feel bigger and work better, from large mirrors and slim furniture to wall storage, see-through pieces and a one-in, one-out rule. Most cost little, and they suit closet-sized entries, narrow halls and apartments with no foyer at all. This post is part of our entryway series, so see the [complete guide to decorating an entryway](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/how-to-decorate-an-entryway) for the full plan.

## How to make a small entry feel bigger

Four principles do most of the work, whatever the size of your space.

Use light. Pale walls, a warm bulb and a large mirror all make a small space feel open. Dark colors can work too, but only with good light and a few pale accents.

Go vertical. When the floor is tight, the walls are your storage. Hooks, shelves and tall mirrors use space you'd otherwise waste, and they draw the eye up.

Show the floor. Furniture on slim legs and a clear patch of floor make a room look larger than furniture sitting flush to the ground.

Edit. In a small space, three well-chosen objects look better than ten. Choose pieces that do two jobs, and let the rest live somewhere else.

Keep as clear a path as you can, ideally 36 inches (91 cm) wide, and check that the front door opens fully before you buy anything.

## 1. Use the walls first

In a small entry, the floor is for walking and the walls are for storage. Mount a rail with hooks for coats and bags, add a narrow shelf above it for hats and baskets, and hang a mirror above the shelf. You get hanging, storing and checking-yourself space without a single piece of furniture on the floor. Fasten rails and shelves into studs or use proper anchors, since coats and bags are heavy. Start at about 60 inches (152 cm) for adult hooks, and add a lower row at 40 to 48 inches if children live in the house.

![Small entryway using wall hooks, a floating shelf and a mirror instead of floor furniture](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-01.jpg)

## 2. Hang a large mirror

A large mirror is the single best trick for a small entry. It reflects light, doubles the sense of depth and gives you a spot to check yourself before you leave. Choose a big one, such as a full-length or a large arch, and lean it against the wall or hang it so the center is near 57 inches (145 cm). Place it opposite a window or a light source if you can, and check what it reflects before you commit, because a mirror that shows a pile of shoes doesn't help. Our guide to [entryway mirror ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-mirror-ideas) covers shapes and sizes.

![Large arched mirror leaning against the wall in a small entryway reflecting a window](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-02.jpg)

## 3. Keep the palette light

Pale walls reflect more light, so a small entry looks larger in warm white, cream, soft greige or pale sage. Keep the trim and the door close to the wall color, so the edges of the room blur, and choose furniture in light wood or white. Add contrast in small doses, with a dark frame, a black hook or a bold print. Use a satin or eggshell finish that wipes clean, and test large samples in your own light, since small entries often get little daylight and colors look different from the chip.

![Small airy entryway with pale walls, a cream runner and light oak furniture](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-03.jpg)

## 4. Choose slim furniture on legs

Furniture that shows the floor underneath looks lighter than furniture that sits flush. Choose a console or a bench with slim legs and an open shelf, and keep the depth shallow. A console 10 to 12 inches deep fits most small entries, and a bench 12 to 14 inches deep works for seating. Avoid bulky pieces with solid fronts to the floor, which make the space feel closed in. Check the door swing and the 36 inch path before you order, and make sure nothing blocks a radiator or a vent.

![Slim black-legged console with an open shelf and a basket in a small entryway](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-04.jpg)

## 5. Swap the console for a floating shelf

If even a slim console is too much, mount a ledge shelf on the wall. A shelf 8 to 10 inches deep holds a lamp, a tray for keys and a small plant, and it leaves the floor completely clear. Mount it at about 34 to 36 inches from the floor, put hooks or a mirror above it and a basket on the floor beneath if you need more storage. Fasten it into studs or with rated anchors, and keep heavy objects near the brackets. It's also one of the easiest options for a rental.

![Floating wooden shelf with a lamp, key tray and plant above hooks in a small entryway](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-05.jpg)

## 6. Add compact seating

A place to sit makes putting on shoes easier, but a full bench may not fit. Tuck a round stool or an ottoman under a console or a shelf, so it takes up no floor when you're not using it. A slim bench 12 inches deep against the wall works in a hallway, and a wall-mounted fold-down seat vanishes when it's not needed. Choose a seat height of 17 to 19 inches, and look for one with storage inside. See our [entryway bench ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-bench-ideas) for more options.

![Round stool tucked under a floating console in a small entryway](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-06.jpg)

## 7. Use a small rug to define the space

A rug makes a tiny entry feel like a room. Choose a small flatweave, such as 2 x 3 feet, or a short runner that leaves bare floor around the edges. A low pile of about 1/4 inch or less lets the door clear, and a non-slip pad keeps it in place. A pattern hides dirt, and a light color makes the space look larger. Avoid a big rug that touches every wall, since it can make a small entry feel cramped. See our [entryway rug ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-rug-ideas) for sizes and materials.

![Small flatweave rug in front of the door in a small entryway with a shoe rack](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-07.jpg)

## 8. Choose pieces that let light through

Open and see-through furniture reduces the visual weight of a small space. Try a console with a glass or acrylic top, a rattan or wire stool, open shelves and an open shoe rack instead of a solid cabinet. Light passes through them, so the entry feels airier. Keep the number of items on open shelves low, since open storage shows everything. Pair a see-through piece with a few solid ones, such as a mirror or a wooden tray, so the entry still has some weight.

![Small entryway with a glass-topped console, rattan stool and open shoe rack](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-08.jpg)

## 9. Light it from the wall

Surfaces are precious in a small entry, so light the space from the wall. A pair of plug-in sconces, or a single one above a shelf, gives a warm glow without using a table or a floor lamp's footprint. Choose a warm 2700K bulb, hide the cords in cord covers painted the wall color, and put a dimmer or a smart plug on one of them. Swapping a harsh overhead bulb for a warm one helps just as much. A small flush-mount fixture keeps the ceiling clean, and a light in a mirror's reflection doubles the glow.

![Small warm sconces above a floating shelf and mirror in a small entryway](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-09.jpg)

## 10. Use the back of the door

The back of the front door is free storage in a space that has none. An over-the-door rack with hooks or fabric pockets holds umbrellas, gloves, dog leashes and keys, and it needs no drilling. Choose one that clears the door frame and doesn't scratch the door, and keep the load light to protect the hinges. A slim mirror on the door adds light as well, if the door can carry the weight. Both options leave the floor and the walls free for everything else.

![Over-the-door organizer holding an umbrella, gloves and keys in a small entryway](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-10.jpg)

## 11. Use the space above and below

Small entries waste a lot of vertical space. Put two baskets under a bench or console for shoes and scarves, and mount a narrow shelf above the door frame for off-season hats and spare baskets. Choose baskets with handles for the lower ones, and label the high ones so you can find things without a ladder. Keep anything heavy low and anything you use every day between knee and shoulder height. Our guide to [small entryway storage ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/small-entryway-storage-ideas) has more ways to fit everything in.

![Small entryway with baskets under a bench and a narrow shelf above the door frame](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-11.jpg)

## 12. Edit hard

In a small space, fewer and larger objects look better than many small ones. On a console or a shelf, stick to three items: a lamp, a tray and one other object, such as a vase or a plant. Leave empty wall and surface around them so the pieces are visible. Move anything that doesn't belong in the entry to another room, and clear the floor of everything but one rug and a pair of shoes. Editing costs nothing, and it often changes a small entry more than any purchase.

![Small entryway console with only a lamp, a tray and a vase](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-12.jpg)

## 13. Hang art tall

In a small entry, art that emphasizes height makes the ceiling feel higher. Hang a tall vertical piece, or stack two or three frames in a narrow column above a shelf or console. Keep the frames to one or two finishes, and use wide mats so the art looks light. Center the group near eye level, around 57 inches (145 cm), and let the top rise toward the ceiling in a narrow space. Avoid a wide, low gallery wall, which can make a small wall feel wider and heavier.

![Three framed prints stacked vertically above a slim console in a small entryway](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-13.jpg)

## 14. Add one bold moment

A small entry is the best place to be brave, because there's so little to commit to. Paint the inside of the front door a deep color such as navy, forest green or charcoal, or put wallpaper on one wall. Keep everything else light and simple, so the bold choice looks deliberate. A brass hook rail or a small mirror against the dark door looks good. In a rental, check your lease before painting, or use a peel-and-stick wallpaper instead.

![Navy front door inside a small entryway with warm white walls and a brass hook rail](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-14.jpg)

## 15. Set a one-in, one-out rule

A small entry can only hold so much, so set a limit. Give each person one or two hooks and three or four pairs of shoes, and when something new comes in, something old goes out. Keep a basket for donations by the door, and empty it when it's full. The rule keeps the entry from filling up again after you've organized it, and it protects the clear path through the space. It's free and it works better than any storage product.

![Small entryway with a clear walkway, two coats on hooks and one pair of shoes on a rack](/assets/uploads/small-entryway-ideas/small-entryway-ideas-15.jpg)

## Small entryway layouts at a glance

Use these as starting points, and measure your own space first.

- No foyer: a small rug, a floating shelf or slim console, a mirror and a row of hooks just inside the door. See our guide to [apartment entryway ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/apartment-entryway-ideas).
- Closet-sized entry, about 4 x 5 feet: hooks on one wall, a mirror on another, a stool and a small rug.
- Hallway entry: a slim console or shelf, a runner and hooks above. See [narrow entryway ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/narrow-entryway-ideas).
- Small foyer: a tall slim cabinet, a narrow bench and a mirror, with a round rug if the floor allows it.

## Small entryway mistakes to avoid

- Choosing furniture that's too big. A bulky piece blocks the walkway, so measure first and keep 36 inches (91 cm) clear.
- Ignoring the door swing. A cabinet or a bench that the door hits is annoying every day.
- Filling every surface. A crowded shelf makes a small entry feel smaller.
- Skipping the light. A dim entry feels tiny, so add a warm lamp or sconces and a mirror.
- Leaving everything on the floor. Move storage up onto the walls and the back of the door.
- Using many small rugs or accessories. Choose one rug and a few larger pieces.
- Going dark with no light sources. Deep colors can look great, but only with a mirror and warm light.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I make a small entryway look bigger?

Use a large mirror, light colors and warm lighting. Choose slim furniture on legs, move storage onto the walls and keep surfaces nearly bare. A clear patch of floor also helps.

### What furniture works in a small entryway?

A slim console or a floating shelf, a stool that tucks under it, wall hooks and a narrow bench. Look for pieces about 10 to 14 inches deep, with legs and open shelves.

### How do I decorate a small entryway with no closet?

Use a wall rail with hooks, a shelf or console for keys and mail, baskets for shoes and an over-the-door rack. Rotate seasonal items into storage elsewhere in the house.

### What color should a small entryway be?

Warm white, cream, greige and soft sage keep a small entry open. If you want a bold color, try the inside of the front door and keep the walls light.
