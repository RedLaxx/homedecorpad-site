The best hacks are not the most complicated ones. They are the ones that fix the specific thing that makes flat-pack furniture look flat-packed: its proportions, its legs, and the fact that it is empty. Here are five that work in a rental and cost very little.

![](</assets/uploads/10. Kitchen Decor & Dining Room Ideas.jpg>)

## 1. The legs swap

The single highest-impact IKEA upgrade there is. Replace the stubby factory legs on a sofa, dresser, nightstand or cabinet with slim tapered wood legs, and the piece instantly reads mid-century rather than mass-market. Tapered oak or walnut, 6–8 inches, screw-in — most pieces have a standard fitting, and adaptor plates are sold for the ones that do not.

## 2. A floating media bench

Mount a low, long cabinet on the wall instead of standing it on the floor. The floor shows underneath, the room reads bigger, and the proportions look built-in. Use a wall-mounting rail and make sure you fix into studs — this one is worth paying someone to install if you are unsure.

:::callout Measure once, drill twice, regret never
Mark your screw holes with painter’s tape rather than a pencil, and tape your drill bit at the depth you need. Two minutes of taping prevents most of the damage amateurs do to flat-pack furniture.
:::

## 3. The styled bookcase

A plain bookcase with the right styling looks custom; the same bookcase styled badly looks like a dorm. Four rules:

- **Half books, half objects.** And stagger them: some stacks flat, some standing.
- **Leave 30% of the space empty.** Empty space is what makes the rest look curated.
- **Add one organic element per shelf.** A plant, a bowl, a vase — even a basket works.
- **Repeat your accent colour at least twice.** Once looks like an accident.

## 4. The ottoman upgrade

Take a plain storage ottoman or cube and give it four small wooden feet from the hardware store. It lifts the piece off the floor, adds a deliberate design detail, and stops it looking like a storage bin. Fifteen minutes, one screwdriver, under $15.

## 5. The curtain rail trick

Pair a cheap ceiling-mounted rail with the longest, cheapest linen-look panels you can find, and hang them wall to wall. This is not a hack so much as architecture: it makes a small living room read taller, hides an awkward ceiling, and costs less than one designer cushion.

## Making flat-pack look custom

:::steps

1. **Fix the legs first.** Proportion is what the eye reads as “expensive.”
2. **Then clear the clutter.** Remove anything on the piece that is not deliberate.
3. **Add one texture and one organic element** — a woven basket, a plant, a wood bowl.
4. **Repeat one colour from the rest of the room.** Flat-pack furniture often arrives with no context; give it one.
5. **Hide the fasteners.** Cover exposed cam locks with small wood plugs, or turn the piece so they face the wall.

:::

## Frequently asked questions

### Do replacement legs actually fit IKEA furniture?

Most tables, sofas and dressers use a standard M8 fitting, and kits include adaptor plates for the exceptions. Measure the existing leg diameter and check the thread before ordering.

### Can I do the floating bench without a drill?

No — wall-mounted furniture needs real fixings into studs or proper anchors with the correct weight rating. It is the one item on this list worth hiring help for.

### What is the cheapest way to make a room look custom?

Free fixes first: [layout](post:living-room-layout-mistakes), light bulbs, and editing surfaces. Then the leg swap. Together they cost under $50 and change a room more than any single piece of furniture.
