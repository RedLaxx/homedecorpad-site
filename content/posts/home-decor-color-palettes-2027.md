The colour trends for the coming season are warm, green-adjacent and heavily wood-based. The good news for anyone on a budget: you do not need to commit to a repaint. Most of these palettes work as textiles, one painted wall, or a few well-chosen accessories against the warm white you already have.

## First, the undertone test

Every palette below is built on warm undertones. To check whether a swatch leans warm or cool, hold it against a plain sheet of white paper in the room where it will live, at three times of day. If it flashes grey or blue next to the paper, it is cooler than it looks in the shop. Warm whites have a yellow-green cast; cool whites have a blue one.

See our [full colour guide](/blog.html#color) for how to test paint in low-light rooms.
:::

## The eight palettes

### 1. Warm Minimal — 60% warm white / 30% oat & camel / 10% terracotta

The safest palette in this list and the one that photographs best. Works in every room. Use terracotta only in small doses: one vase, one cushion, one piece of art.

### 2. Sage & Cream Kitchen — 60% cream / 30% sage / 10% warm oak

Sage cabinetry with cream walls and visible wood is the look driving most kitchen saves right now. If you cannot change cabinets, paint one island panel or add sage textiles and a wood board lean.

### 3. Butter Yellow & Warm White — 60% warm white / 30% butter / 10% black

Butter yellow is the trend with the longest legs because it sits inside the warm-neutral family. Use it on one wall, a headboard, or curtains. The 10% black is what keeps it from looking like a nursery.

### 4. Moody Blue & Brass — 60% soft navy / 25% warm white / 15% brass & oak

For a study, a dining room, or the wall behind a bed. Deep blue needs warm metals to stay rich — brass, aged bronze, or unlacquered copper. Cool silver will make it look cold.

### 5. Dark Cottage — 60% deep green / 25% warm white / 15% antique wood

The moodiest palette here, and the fastest way to make a small room feel grown-up. Best on lower cabinetry, a powder room, or a mudroom wall. Always pair with a warm white above so the ceiling breathes.

### 6. Aubergine Accent — 70% oat / 20% aubergine / 10% warm wood

Aubergine on an island, a built-in, or a single bathroom wall. It is a plum that behaves like a neutral when surrounded by oat and wood. Resist matching it with grey.

### 7. White Oak & Black — 50% white oak / 30% warm white / 20% black hardware

Clean, architectural and surprisingly forgiving. Use matte black in hardware, frames and lighting rather than painted surfaces. In spaces that already have cool metals, switch the black to rubbed bronze to avoid a clash.

### 8. Earthy Maximal — 40% warm white / 30% terracotta / 20% olive / 10% mustard

For the reader who finds minimalism boring. Keep the base warm white and let pattern, plants and books carry the rest. Repeat each colour at least twice in the room or it will look accidental.

:::callout Proportion is the whole game
Use 60% of the dominant colour, 30% of the support, and 10% of the accent — by *visible surface area*, not number of objects. If a palette feels off, it is almost always the accent taking more than 10%.
:::

## Making it work in low light

- Warm white on all ceilings and trim, always. This includes north-facing rooms.
- Choose the mid-tone for the largest upholstered piece, not the walls, so the light has something warm to land on.
- Add a third light source before you change any colour — a room lit properly reads richer even in the same paint.
- If a room only gets afternoon light, avoid the moody palettes at full strength. Use 20% instead of 60%.

## How to test before you commit

:::steps
1. **Paint two large samples on cardboard** — not the tiny swatches. Use a 12-inch square, two coats.
2. **Move them around the room** morning, afternoon and under lamplight. Watch how the colour changes when the sun goes down.
3. **Look at the sample in shade, not sunlight.** The shadowed wall is what you will actually see most of the day.
4. **Check the trim together with the wall colour.** A warm white wall with a cool white trim is the most common expensive mistake in this list.
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### Which palette adds the most resale value?

Warm Minimal. It is the least polarising and works in listing photography. Keep the bolder palettes to rooms you actually use, like a study or powder room.

### Can I mix two palettes in one home?

Yes, if they share the same dominant neutral. Use Warm Minimal as the base in living areas and choose one bolder accent palette for one room.

### Is grey out?

Cool grey is fading, warm grey is not. If you already have grey floors, add oat, oak and brass to warm the room rather than repainting everything.
