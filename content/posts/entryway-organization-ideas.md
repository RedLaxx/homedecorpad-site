Good entryway organization makes the right thing the easy thing. If a hook is where your hand naturally goes, the coat gets hung. This guide has 15 entryway organization ideas for shoes, keys, bags, mail, pets and kids, from hooks and trays to cubbies, pegboards and a five-minute reset. Many work in small entries and cost little. This post is part of our entryway series, so see the [complete guide to decorating an entryway](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/how-to-decorate-an-entryway) for the full plan.

## Start with a system, not a purchase

Before you buy a single basket, take ten minutes to see what actually lands at your door. List the shoes, coats, bags, keys, mail, pet gear and anything else that collects there. You'll usually find that two or three categories cause most of the mess. Then follow four simple rules.

Give every category a home within one step of the door. If putting something away takes two motions, it won't happen on a busy morning.

Store things by how often you use them. Daily items live at the door, occasional ones go in a closet or on a high shelf, and off-season ones go somewhere else entirely.

Match the storage to the person. Hooks at a child's height, a basket they can reach and a label they can read all make it likelier that the system gets used.

Keep a reset habit. Five minutes a week puts everything back and shows you what isn't working.

## 1. Give each person a hook

One hook per person is the simplest organization system there is. Mount a rail with a hook for each member of the household, plus two for guests, and add a small name tag or a color above each one. Hang adult hooks about 60 inches (152 cm) from the floor, and add a lower row at 40 to 48 inches for children, so they can hang up their own things. Fasten the rail into studs or use proper anchors, since coats and bags are heavy. Hooks beat a closet for daily items, because hanging takes one motion and the item stays in sight.

## 2. Add a shoe rack or boot tray

Shoes are the biggest single source of entry clutter, so give them a dedicated spot. A two-tier rack holds the pairs you wear every day, and a rubber boot tray catches wet or muddy ones. Keep about three or four pairs per person at the door, and store the rest in a closet. Choose an open rack with a slatted shelf, so shoes can air and dry. Put it against the wall near where you sit to put shoes on, and wipe the tray out once a week. In a very small entry, a wall-mounted shoe shelf frees the floor completely.

![Two-tier wooden shoe rack and a boot tray with rain boots in an entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-organization-ideas/entryway-organization-ideas-02.jpg)

## 3. Set up a key and mail station

Keys and mail go missing because they have no fixed home. Put a small tray or a wall organizer near the door, with hooks for keys and slots for letters. Sort the mail standing at the door: recycle the junk right away, put what needs action in the slot and deal with the rest within a few days. If wall space is tight, a bowl or a tray on a console works just as well, and it keeps loose change and sunglasses in one place. ## 4. Use baskets for bags and gear

Baskets are forgiving storage, since they hold odd-shaped things and hide the contents. Use one for bags, one for hats and gloves and one for umbrellas, and give each a small tag. Choose baskets with handles so they pull out easily, and put them on a low shelf, under a bench or in a cubby. Woven seagrass and rattan look good, and fabric bins are lighter. Avoid one big basket for everything, which quickly becomes a dumping ground. Line the inside with fabric if the contents might snag.

![Three labeled woven baskets holding bags, hats and umbrellas on an entryway shelf](/assets/uploads/entryway-organization-ideas/entryway-organization-ideas-04.jpg)

## 5. Install a wall-mounted organizer

A wall-mounted organizer keeps the floor clear, which matters most in a small entry. Look for a rail or board that combines hooks, a small shelf for keys and a basket or bin on a bracket. Mount it where your hand naturally goes, and fasten it into studs or with rated anchors. A narrow unit works well in a hallway, since it sticks out only a few inches. Use the shelf for daily items such as keys and sunglasses, and the hooks for the coat and bag you use now. Our guide to [small entryway storage ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/small-entryway-storage-ideas) has more wall-mounted options.

## 6. Hide a charging spot

Chargers and cords make an entry look messy fast. Set up a charging spot in a drawer or a lidded box on the console, with a power strip inside and a cable for each device. Cut a small opening at the back for the power cord, or use a box made for the purpose. Keep the chargers uncovered and ventilated, use a quality power strip and don't stack anything on top. Add a small tray inside for keys, so phones and keys stay together and you grab both on the way out.

![Open console drawer with a tidy charging station for phones and keys](/assets/uploads/entryway-organization-ideas/entryway-organization-ideas-06.jpg)

## 7. Use the back of the door

The back of the front door is free storage. An over-the-door rack with hooks or fabric pockets holds umbrellas, gloves, dog leashes and keys without using any floor or wall space. Choose one that won't scratch the door and that clears the frame, since a rack that's too thick stops the door closing. Keep the load light to protect the hinges. It's a good option for rentals, because it needs no drilling.

![Over-the-door organizer with hooks and pockets holding an umbrella, gloves and keys](/assets/uploads/entryway-organization-ideas/entryway-organization-ideas-07.jpg)

## 8. Choose a shoe cabinet with tilt-out drawers

If you'd rather not look at shoes at all, a slim cabinet with flip-down drawers hides them behind doors. Each drawer tilts open to show a row of pairs, then closes flat. Many are only about 7 to 12 inches deep, which suits a narrow hallway. Check how many pairs it holds, and use the top for a lamp, a tray and a mirror above. Anchor tall units to the wall with an anti-tip strap. ## 9. Pick a bench with storage

A storage bench gives you a seat and a hiding place in one piece. A lift-top bench hides gloves, hats and off-season shoes inside, and an open-shelf bench keeps everyday pairs in view and within reach. Look for a safety hinge that holds the lid open, so it can't drop on small fingers, and a seat height of 17 to 19 inches. Add hooks above it, and the whole drop zone fits in a few square feet. Our guide to [entryway bench ideas](https://redlaxx.github.io/homedecorpad-site/blog/entryway/entryway-bench-ideas) covers 15 options.

## 10. Create a dog station

Dogs bring their own gear: leashes, waste bags, treats, towels and sometimes a harness. Give all of it one spot near the door. A hook for the leash at your height, a small basket for waste bags, a towel and a brush, a lidded jar for treats and a water bowl on a mat keep everything within reach. Seal the treats so they don't attract pests, and keep anything small or chewable out of a puppy's reach. A rubber mat under the bowl and the paws catches drips and mud.

![Dog station by the front door with a leash hook, basket, treat jar and water bowl](/assets/uploads/entryway-organization-ideas/entryway-organization-ideas-10.jpg)

## 11. Set up a kids' drop zone

Children can run a good system if it's built for their size. Give each child their own cubby or bin and a low hook, and use a picture or a color for those who can't read yet. Teach one simple routine, such as backpack on the hook and shoes in the cubby, and keep it to three items per child so it stays easy. A low bench gives them somewhere to sit. Check the cubbies every week or two, and pass on outgrown shoes and coats so the space doesn't fill up.

![Kids' drop zone with cubbies, low hooks, small backpacks and a bench](/assets/uploads/entryway-organization-ideas/entryway-organization-ideas-11.jpg)

## 12. Use a slim rolling cart

A slim rolling cart gives you storage where there's no wall space or built-in. Choose a three-tier cart about 10 to 12 inches wide, and use the bottom for shoes, the middle for baskets and the top for daily items such as keys and gloves. Lock the wheels so it doesn't drift into the walkway, and roll it out of the way when you vacuum. It's a good option for rentals, since it moves with you, and it works in an apartment entry that has no closet.

![Slim three-tier rolling cart holding shoes, a basket and a key tray in a small entryway](/assets/uploads/entryway-organization-ideas/entryway-organization-ideas-12.jpg)

## 13. Hang a pegboard or grid wall

A pegboard or grid wall lets you change your system as your needs change. Mount a panel in wood, metal or painted board, and add hooks, small shelves, baskets and holders where you need them. It suits a household with a mix of items, such as hats, keys, tote bags and dog gear. Anchor the panel into studs, and keep heavier items low and near the supports. Paint it the wall color for a quiet look, or a bold color to make it a feature.

![Wood pegboard wall with hooks, shelves and baskets above an entryway bench](/assets/uploads/entryway-organization-ideas/entryway-organization-ideas-13.jpg)

## 14. Rotate your seasonal items

Off-season items take up space you need every day. Keep only the current season's coats, boots and accessories at the door, and store the rest in labeled bins on a high shelf, in a closet or under a bed. Swap twice a year, and use the swap to donate what you didn't wear. Write the contents on the bin, so you can find a scarf in January without opening four boxes. ## 15. Build a five-minute reset

A system only works if you keep it up. Pick one time each week, such as Sunday evening, and spend five minutes putting shoes back, emptying the tray, sorting mail, shaking out the rug and wiping the surfaces. Add a two-minute daily habit, such as keys in the tray and shoes on the rack. A reset also shows you what isn't working: if a basket always overflows, it needs to be bigger, or fewer things need to live there. Ask everyone in the household to do their part.

## What belongs by the door, and what doesn't

A quick sort keeps the entry from becoming storage for everything.

By the door: this week's shoes, the coats you're wearing now, your daily bag, keys, mail that needs action and an umbrella.

Somewhere else: off-season coats and boots, shoes you rarely wear, bulk supplies, packages you've opened and anything that lives better in the closet, the garage or a bedroom.

If you're not sure, ask whether you used it in the last two weeks. If you didn't, it can live somewhere less valuable than your front door.

## Entryway organization mistakes to avoid

- Buying storage before sorting. You can't pick the right size until you know what you own, so sort first.
- Too few hooks. If there's no free hook, the coat goes on the bench, so give every person one and add guest hooks.
- Hiding everything. Closed storage is tidy, but daily items need to be easy to reach.
- Using one big basket. It becomes a dumping ground, so use several small ones with tags.
- Hanging hooks too high for children. If they can't reach them, they won't use them.
- Overloading wall mounts. Coats and bags are heavy, so fasten hooks and racks into studs or use rated anchors.
- Skipping the reset. A system with no maintenance drifts back to clutter within weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I organize shoes in a small entryway?

Keep three or four pairs per person at the door on a slim rack, a wall shelf or a tilt-out cabinet, and store the rest in a closet. Use a boot tray for wet pairs, and rotate seasonal shoes twice a year.

### How many hooks do I need in an entryway?

One per person, plus two for guests. Add a lower row at 40 to 48 inches (102 to 122 cm) for children, and mount adult hooks about 60 inches (152 cm) from the floor.

### How do I stop mail piling up in the entry?

Give it one home, such as a slot or a tray, and sort it standing at the door. Recycle junk right away, and deal with the rest at a set time each week.

### How often should I reset my entryway?

A five-minute weekly reset keeps most entries under control, along with a two-minute daily habit like keys in the tray. Do a bigger sort at the start of each season.
