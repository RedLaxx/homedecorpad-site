Why Measure Before You Shop

Styled product photos don’t show your door swing, baseboards, or weekday shoe pile.

A console table that looks slim online can block an inward-opening front door or pinch your walkway below a comfortable 36 inches. This planner walks you through the exact sequence interior designers use—on paper, at true 1:16 scale—before you spend a dollar.

Protect the 36 in clear walkway

Record usable width, depth, trim thickness, outlets, and the full arc of your front door. Plot everything on the landscape 1:16 grid where 1 square = 4 inches.

STEP 02 · RIGHT-SIZE STORAGE

Plan for rainy Tuesdays, not empty halls

Count the real weekday and guest load—shoes, coats, backpacks, totes, umbrellas, pet gear, and keys—and turn that number into exact hook, bin, and shoe-pair targets.

Questions & Answers

Everything to know before you print

How do I make sure the 1:16 layout grid and cut-outs print at the exact scale? +

In your PDF viewer’s print dialog, choose 100% or Actual Size and uncheck “Fit to Page” or “Shrink Oversized Pages.” Once printed, measure the 1 in test bar on Page 03 and the 1 in test square on Page 04 with a standard ruler. At 100% scale, 1 inch on paper equals 16 inches in your room (each small square = 4 inches).

Is this an interactive fillable PDF or a printable write-in planner? +

It is a printable, write-in worksheet PDF (not an interactive fillable form). It is designed to be printed on US Letter paper (8.5 × 11 in) so you can sketch walls with a pencil, cut out and rearrange the 1:16 furniture footprints on the grid, and take your measurements to the store.

What if my entryway is just a wall next to the front door with no separate foyer? +

Page 01’s Entry-Type Quiz specifically covers No-Foyer spaces (where the front door opens straight into a living room) as well as Narrow, Small, and Large entries—showing you how to carve out a wall-based drop zone while keeping the main room and door swing clear.

Are the 36 in walkway and 57 in mirror height building codes? +

No—they are practical interior-design starting points and planning targets, not universal building codes. The planner gives you space to adjust every measurement for your household’s height, sightlines, mobility needs, and wall conditions.
